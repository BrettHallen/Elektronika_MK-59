# Elektronika MK-59<br>Электроника МК-59<br>Родон МК-04
Information about the Soviet/Ukrainian desktop calculator known as the Elektronika MK–59, then later the Rodon MK-04.<br>

## History

- 1967–1971: On 27 September 1967 the Soviet government decided to build an electronics plant in Ivano-Frankivsk (in what was then the Ukrainian SSR). It started in 1971 as the Позитрон (Positron) plant, growing out of a workshop of the Minsk semiconductor plant. Most of its early work was defence electronics and was secret.
- Late 1980s: It had about 10,000 workers and made around 100 million integrated circuits a year, of 150 types. It also made over 40 million roubles’ worth of consumer goods a year, including four types of calculator and eight types of telephone.
- Rodon: Around that time it became the core of a production association called Родон (Rodon). Its design bureau, СКТБ «Орізон» (Orizon), designed the МК-59, МК-60 and МК-71 calculators, which the Positron plant built.
- 1985: The Электроника МК-59 was introduced. It was a 16-digit desktop calculator for planning, accounting and statistics, made both for the Soviet economy and for export. Its reed-switch keys and 16-digit vacuum-fluorescent display stood out among Soviet calculators of the time. One collector’s site describes it as essentially an improved desktop version of the earlier Б3-05М.
- МК-59М (1990s): A revised version had a rubber-membrane keyboard instead of reed switches, and a slightly changed circuit with an added К172БП1 delay chip. Wikipedia gives no source for this.
- Родон МК 04 (1993–1994): After privatisation the calculator was sold under the Rodon brand. It kept the reed-switch keys and the same display, and the box said «Виготовлено в Україні» (“Made in Ukraine”). It was one of a small Rodon range alongside the МК01 scientific calculator (based on the МК-71) and the МК 05 solar pocket model (based on the МК-60).
- 1993 onwards: It was privatised as the joint-stock company ВАТ «Родон», but it couldn’t compete with imports. It declined, its equipment was stripped, and parts of the site were later reused by other businesses.
